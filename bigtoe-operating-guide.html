BigToe

Operating Guide — everything you need to set up, connect, and play with your BigToe MIDI foot controller.

Introduction

BigToe is a unique MIDI foot controller built for musicians using software effects or any MIDI-enabled equipment, giving you all the controls you want, anywhere you want them.

Unlike traditional controllers with a limited number of buttons, BigToe gives you a physical touchpoint for as many of your software effects as you like, laid out just like physical pedals.

BigToe's patented solution uses RFID and proprietary technology to help you break free of banks and presets. It has no noisy switches, is virtually spill-proof, and its ultra-lightweight design makes it perfect for the mobile musician.

Components

Pedal Pads/RFID tags

Pads holds your RFID tags. Each RFID tag is a "pedal" that triggers your software effects.

Device

A main compartment holding a microprocessor and battery, attached to an RFID reader.

Sock

Slips over your shoe. The Device sits in the top pocket, the RFID reader tucks under your toes.

Setup

You set up and monitor your BigToe through Wi-Fi and a standard web browser interface. This is where you add new pedals, and edit them later.

  1. Turn on the device. The LED will flash yellow, indicating it's running but not yet connected to send MIDI messages to your software effects.
  2. Go to your computing device's Wi-Fi configuration and you'll see your BigToe listed as a network device, named in the format BigToe-xxxxxxxxxxxx. Select it and use the default Wi-Fi password 1234567890 (You can change both the name and password later). The LED will change to solid yellow.
  3. Open a web browser and go to bigtoe.local — the BigToe screen will appear.

Creating your Pedals

Your BigToe comes with 16 Pedals that have been preloaded. Click (laptop) or tap (touchscreen) a Pedal name to edit or delete it. If you delete the Pedal, you can add it back from the menu.

From the menu, select New. The LED will rapidly flash white, indicating it's ready for you to select an RFID tag — you will have about 20 seconds to tap the reader to it (you can always just do it again if you miss the window). The Edit Window will appear and the LED returns to solid yellow. Define the pedal's information in the Edit Window; the same process applies when editing an existing pedal.

To edit an existing Pedal, click the pencil icon ✎ and the Edit Window will appear.

Edit Window fields

  • ImageWith the supplied RFID tags, the screen image is set automatically from your tag by default. Any other tag defaults to a white circle — click the image to choose your own from your computer. Print it and stick it on the tag if you like. Or draw on a card, take a picture and upload that image. You can have a lot of fun customizing your layout.
  • NameUp to 15 characters, e.g. "Chorus," "Overdrive," "Reverb."
  • GroupEnter a name for a group this Pedal belongs to up to 15 characters. BigToe ensures only one pedal in a Group is "on" at a time — useful for several pedals of one effect class, like four delay types, or amps. Turning one on in the group automatically turns the others off.
  • PadWhich pad the pedal appears in. Defaults to the first available slot.
  • BkgndBlack or White, matching your RFID card. Supplied tags print the icon on a black background, but standard white cards work too.
  • Fire OnPress or Release — fire the MIDI event when you first tap the tag, or when you lift the reader away from it.

Then define the MIDI event for this pedal. One row is visible by default — click Show More to reveal all 5 event rows, or Show Less to collapse back to one.

Event rows

  • ActiveThe first row is active by default. Events only fire when this toggle is set (green).
  • Mode
    • Footswitch — classic stomp-box action - one tap turns the effect on, a second turns it off.
    • Momentary — on when tapped, off when released.
    • Expression — sets values for a continuous controller and can increment or decrement that value with each press. Good for simulating faders and knobs.
    • Trigger — fires an event without Footswitch mode's state management. Also supports increment/decrement per press — useful for paging through presets and banks.
  • TypeBigToe sends CC (Control Change), PC (Program Change), and NOTE messages.
  • ChannelOne of the 16 MIDI channels.
  • CC / PC / NoteThe CC, PC or NOTE value.
  • ValueSet automatically for Footswitch and Momentary. For Expression pedals, set a value from 0–127.
  • + / −For Expression and Trigger modes, increment or decrement the pedal's current value.
  • BumpFor Expression pedals, how much each press increments or decrements the value. Set a value from 0–127.

Click Add (or Update when editing an existing Pedal ) to finish — your pedal appears on screen. Once your pedals are defined, use your effect software's MIDI settings to map its controls to the events you've created.

Setting the pad name

Click the Pad Name to open a dialog where you can set a name for the pad — a visual aid for organizing your pedals.

Connecting to your effects software or MIDI instrument

BigToe connects to your MIDI software or hardware over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE). Once connected, the LED turns solid blue, indicating you're ready to send MIDI messages.

Note — connect your browser window to the BigToe before Bluetooth, in order to enable BLE.

Bluetooth

macOS

Open Audio MIDI Setup, then the Bluetooth Configuration window. Your device will show as active — click Connect and watch the LED shift from solid yellow to solid blue.

iOS

Many MIDI-enabled apps, like Loopy Pro, include Bluetooth connection controls — follow your app's instructions. If your app doesn't have a Bluetooth connection control, use a third-party app like nRF Connect.

Windows

See the Windows BLE MIDI Setup Guide for step-by-step instructions.

Hardware devices

Devices without built-in Bluetooth can connect via third-party wireless adapters, such as CME's WIDI Master. Once plugged into your MIDI hardware, the adapter finds and pairs with your BigToe automatically. Check your adapter's manual for details.

Using BigToe

With your pedals defined and your MIDI transport connected (blue light on), you're ready to play. A tap of the tag with the reader under your toe is all it takes to trigger your MIDI event.

The status light

The LED on BigToe shows what it's doing at a glance.

  • Flashing yellowBigToe is on, with nothing connected yet.
  • Solid yellowYour browser is connected to BigToe's WiFi; Bluetooth MIDI isn't connected yet.
  • Solid blueBluetooth MIDI is connected — you're ready to play.
  • WhiteA pedal was turned on. Shows for 2 seconds, then returns to blue.
  • White, blinkingA pedal was turned off. Blinks for 2 seconds, then returns to blue.
  • Flashing whiteWaiting for you to tap a tag, e.g. when defining a new pedal. Returns to yellow or blue once the tag is read.

The monitor screen

The BigToe monitor is where you define and edit pedals, and monitor effects in real time. It also acts as a control surface, so you can trigger events directly from the interface.

Across the top of the screen:

  • Menu — the hamburger icon reveals the main menu functions.
  • Device Name — the default name, or the one you've set.
  • Battery — battery health; roughly 6 hours of use per charge, via the supplied USB-C cable.
  • BigToe icon — well,… why not?

Menu functions

Pedals

  • New — define a Pedal for a new RFID tag.
  • Find — locate the Pedal a specific tag is linked to.
  • Clear All — clear every Pedal definition.
  • Pedal Size +/− — resize the Pedal icons on screen.

Save / Load Pedals & Pads

  • Save — write your current Pedal and Pad layout to a file, handy for separate sets per gig or instrument. Saved to your browser's download folder.
  • Load — restore a saved file. Current Pedals are deleted and replaced.

Settings

  • Pad Layout — customize Pad order, how many appear per row, and which are shown.
  • Manage User Images — see available space, load images from your computing device, and delete unused images to free up space.
  • Set Device Name — The default name for your device is "BigToe-" + the MAC address (network ID). Replace the default name with your own up to 20 characters.
  • Set Wi-Fi Password — change it from the default 1234567890.

Drag and drop

Pedals can be rearranged with a drag-and-drop action:

  • Laptop — click the pedal body and drag. Its background turns gray while dragging.
  • Touchscreen — tap and hold the pedal body until it turns gray, then drag.

Drop the pedal at its new spot — every other pedal shifts right to make room.

The sock

The machine-washable sock stretches easily to fit your shoe size. Integrated pockets keep your BigToe device snug and secure, and the non-slip backing keeps you steady too. It's so light and close-fitting you may forget you're wearing it.

Pedal pads and RFID

The pads for your RFID tags are built for flexibility:

  • Place them anywhere on stage that's convenient — around mic stands, or to fit your floor configuration.
  • Define duplicate pedals to place at different points on stage.
  • Swap tag positions as needed, then drag and drop them on your BigToe monitor to match.
  • Soft yet durable, flexible, and lightweight — they fit easily in a gig bag, guitar case, backpack, or even a pocket.

RFID tag types

Pads fit standard CR80 RFID cards. Any of these high-frequency (13.56 MHz) tag types work with BigToe:

MIFARE Classic — recommended MIFARE Ultralight NTAG 213 NTAG 215

MIFARE Classic–compatible tags have smaller unique identifiers, making reading and pedal identification a few milliseconds faster than other tag types.
